Imagine you're a Space X engineer on a tiny island in the Pacific Ocean, in the Marshall Islands. It's September twenty eight, two thousand eight. In front of you is a skinny rocket called Falcon one. It has already failed three times. The first one caught fire and crashed. The second one started spinning and shut down too early. On the third, the two halves of the rocket smashed into each other. Your boss knows a fourth failure would be game over. And your boss is in deep trouble. His car company is running out of money. His marriage is ending. He has put almost everything he owns into this rocket and those cars. He's thirty seven years old. His name is Elon Musk.
So how does a bullied kid from South Africa end up here, betting everything on one rocket? And how does he go from almost broke to the first trillionaire in history? Coming up, a twelve-year-old who sells his own video game. A million-dollar car crash with no insurance. A man fired on his honeymoon. A car sent into space with a dummy at the wheel. A forty four billion dollar website. And a chainsaw on a stage. But first, we go back to South Africa, nineteen seventy one.
Elon Musk is born on June twenty eighth, nineteen seventy one, in Pretoria. His mother, Maye, is a model and a dietitian. His father, Errol, is an engineer. And little Elon? He reads. All day. When he runs out of books, he reads the encyclopedia. He gets so lost in his own thoughts that he doesn't hear people talking to him. His parents even think he might be deaf. He isn't. He's just thinking.
School is a different story. Elon is small, shy and awkward, and the other kids notice. One day, a group of boys throws him down a flight of concrete stairs. They beat him until he blacks out, and he ends up in the hospital. At home, things aren't easy either. His parents split up when he's young.
As a teenager, he finds a book that changes how he sees the world. The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy. It's a funny science fiction story. In it, a giant computer says the answer to life, the universe and everything is forty two. The problem is, nobody knows what the question was. Young Elon takes a lesson from it. The hardest part is often asking the right question. Remember this book. It comes back in a strange way.
He also teaches himself to code. At twelve, he builds a space video game called Blastar. He sells it to a computer magazine for five hundred dollars. Years later, he jokes that it was "a trivial game, but better than Flappy Bird." You can still play it online today.
Now, you might have heard that Elon grew up rich, thanks to his father's emerald mine. His biographer says there was no mine. In the nineteen eighties, his father traded a small plane for some emeralds, and that business later fell apart. When Elon leaves South Africa at seventeen, he has about four thousand dollars.
It's nineteen eighty nine, and he flies to Canada, where his mother was born. He sleeps in a youth hostel, sharing a room with five strangers, and takes odd jobs. He studies at Queen's University in Canada. Then he moves to the University of Pennsylvania in the U S, to study physics and economics. In nineteen ninety five, he gets into Stanford to do a P H D. He quits after two days. The internet is exploding, and he wants to be part of it.
With his younger brother Kimbal, he starts a company called Zip two. It puts maps and business listings online for newspapers. Money is so tight that Elon lives in the office and showers at the local Y M C A. Then, in nineteen ninety nine, the computer giant Compaq buys Zip two for more than three hundred million dollars. Elon's share? twenty two million. He's twenty seven years old.
So what does he buy? A McLaren F one, one of the fastest cars on Earth, for about a million dollars. A year later, he's driving it with a friend named Peter Thiel. Thiel asks what the car can do. Elon says, "Watch this." He floors it. The car spins and hits a bank of earth. It flies into the air and lands back on the road, wrecked. Nobody's hurt. And the insurance? There isn't any.
But here's the strange part. Most people with twenty two million dollars would relax. Elon puts ten million of it into his next company. And this becomes his rule. Whenever he wins, he bets it all again. Remember it, because it explains almost everything he does.
The new company is an online bank called X dot com. Yes, X. Remember that letter. It keeps coming back. X dot com joins with a rival company, and together they become PayPal. Elon is the boss. Then, in September two thousand, he finally goes on his honeymoon. He's already put it off for months because of work. While he's away in Australia, the board fires him. He rushes back, but it's too late.
A few months later, on a safari in South Africa, he catches malaria. He comes within hours of dying. He spends ten days in intensive care, and it takes him about five months to recover. He learns a lesson. In his words, "Vacations will kill you."
In two thousand two, eBay buys PayPal for one point five billion dollars. Elon walks away with more than one hundred fifty million. And other early PayPal people? They go on to start YouTube, LinkedIn and Yelp. People call them the PayPal Mafia. That same year, tragedy strikes. His first son, Nevada, dies at just ten weeks old.
So what does he do with all that money? Remember the rule? He goes all in. Elon has a dream. He wants humans to live on Mars. He says humans need to become a multi-planet species, in case something terrible ever happens to Earth. He even jokes, "I'd like to die on Mars. Just not on impact." First, he wants to send a small greenhouse there, to get people excited about space again. So he flies to Russia to buy old missiles to use as rockets. The Russians want eight million dollars or more for each one. On the flight home, he does the math. The raw materials in a rocket cost only about two to three percent of its price. So he thinks, why not build rockets myself? He calls this thinking from first principles. Break a problem down to its basic parts, then build it back up. In two thousand two, he starts Space X.
Two years later, he puts money into a small electric car company called Tesla. And here's a myth. You might have heard that Elon founded Tesla. He didn't. Two engineers, Martin Eberhard and Marc Tarpenning, started it in two thousand three. Elon was its biggest early investor and became its chairman. Later, after a legal fight, he's allowed to call himself a co-founder. Tesla's first car, the Roadster, is a sports car. It runs on thousands of small batteries, like the ones in laptops.
Now, back to that island. By two thousand eight, everything is going wrong. Falcon one has failed three times. Tesla is burning through money. The world economy is crashing. His marriage ends in divorce. Elon later calls it "the worst year of my life." Almost all his PayPal money is now inside Space X and Tesla. By the end of the year, he's living on loans from friends.
Then, on September twenty eight, two thousand eight, the fourth Falcon one lifts off. And this time, it works. It becomes the first privately built liquid-fueled rocket to reach orbit. Elon calls it "one of the best days of my life." Remember that engineer on the island? He just watched history.
But Space X still needs money. On December twenty three, NASA calls. Space X has won a one point six billion dollar contract to fly cargo to the International Space Station. Elon is so shocked he can barely hold the phone. He blurts out that he loves them. Years later, he says that contract saved Space X. The next day, Christmas Eve, Tesla's investors agree to put in more money. Tesla survives too. In a few months, he goes from almost losing everything to saving both companies.
From here, things move fast. In two thousand ten, Tesla goes public. In two thousand twelve, its Model S comes out, and a top car magazine names it Car of the Year. That same year, Space X's Dragon becomes the first private spacecraft to reach the space station.
Then Space X tries something everyone says is crazy. Landing rockets. Back then, every rocket was thrown away after one flight. Imagine throwing away a plane after every trip. Space X's first landing tries end in fireballs. But on December twenty one, two thousand fifteen, a Falcon nine booster flies back and lands standing up.
In two thousand eighteen, Space X launches its giant Falcon Heavy rocket. For the test cargo, Elon sends his own red Tesla sports car into space. At the wheel is a dummy in a spacesuit. The radio plays David Bowie's "Space Oddity." And on the car's screen are two words: "Don't panic." Remember that book he read as a kid? Those are the words on the cover of the Hitchhiker's Guide. That car is still out there today, circling the Sun.
In twenty twenty, Space X becomes the first private company to fly astronauts to the space station. Today, Space X launches more rockets than any country on Earth. And its Starlink satellites, thousands of them, beam internet to people all over the world. When Russia invades Ukraine in two thousand twenty two, Starlink helps keep Ukraine online.
But it's not all smooth. In two thousand eighteen, Tesla struggles to build its Model three fast enough. Elon calls it "production hell" and sleeps on the factory floor. That same year, he tweets that he has "funding secured" to buy out Tesla's shareholders. U S regulators say that wasn't true. Without admitting wrongdoing, he and Tesla each pay a twenty million dollar fine, and he steps down as Tesla's chairman.
In twenty nineteen, he shows off the Cybertruck, a steel pickup truck with "armor glass." On stage, his designer throws a metal ball at the window to show how strong it is. It cracks. They try the back window. That cracks too.
Still, Tesla's stock keeps climbing. By January twenty twenty one, Elon Musk is the richest person in the world. Later that year, Tesla is worth more than a trillion dollars. Along the way, he even names one of his sons X. And in twenty twenty one, he hosts the comedy show Saturday Night Live. On stage, he says he has Asperger's syndrome. He jokes that he's the first person with Asperger's to host the show. "Or at least the first to admit it."
He keeps starting companies too. One digs tunnels under cities. Another, called Neuralink, puts chips in human brains. In twenty twenty four, a paralyzed man named Noland Arbaugh plays chess on a computer just by thinking. Elon also helped start Open A I, the company that later made Chat G P T. Then he left, and in twenty twenty three he starts his own A I company, X A I. Its chatbot is called Grok, a word from an old science fiction novel. At Tesla, he announces a humanoid robot. To show it off, a man in a robot costume dances on stage.
In twenty twenty two, he makes his biggest bet yet. He offers forty four billion dollars for Twitter. Then he tries to back out, saying the site has too many fake accounts. Twitter sues him, and in the end, he buys it anyway. In October twenty twenty two, he walks into Twitter's headquarters carrying a bathroom sink, and posts, "Let that sink in." Within months, he cuts about eighty percent of the staff. He tells the ones who stay to be "extremely hardcore," or leave. And in twenty twenty three, Twitter gets a new name. Can you guess it? X. Remember X dot com, his bank from nineteen ninety nine? He had already bought that web address back.
Then he goes all in on something new. Politics. In July twenty twenty four, minutes after someone tries to kill Donald Trump at a rally, Musk endorses him. He spends about two hundred seventy seven million dollars to help elect Trump and other Republicans. That makes him the biggest donor of the twenty twenty four election.
After Trump wins, Musk is put in charge of a new team called doge. That's short for the Department of Government Efficiency. Its job is to cut government spending. At first, he talks about saving two trillion dollars. Later, he lowers that goal. At a conference in February twenty twenty five, he waves a chainsaw on stage, a gift from Argentina's president. doge cancels contracts and cuts thousands of government jobs. His fans cheer. Protesters gather outside Tesla stores, and Tesla's sales drop.
At the end of May twenty twenty five, he leaves doge. Days later, he and Trump have a huge public fight over Trump's big spending bill. Trump threatens to cancel Space X's government contracts. Musk says Trump wouldn't have won without him. Musk even announces his own political party, the America Party. But it never gets going. By twenty twenty six, he's back. In the midterm elections, he's once again the biggest single donor, backing Republicans. And in October, Trump gives him the National Medal of Science.
Meanwhile, the rockets keep getting bigger. Starship is the biggest rocket ever built, made to one day carry people to Mars. Its first test flight, in twenty twenty three, ends in a huge explosion. Space X calls it a "rapid unscheduled disassembly." But they keep trying. In October twenty twenty four, its giant booster comes back from the edge of space. It's as tall as a twenty story building. Two huge metal arms on the launch tower catch it in midair. Engineers call them the chopsticks.
In twenty twenty five, Tesla starts testing robotaxis, cars with no driver, in Austin, Texas. That November, Tesla's shareholders approve a new pay deal for him. It could give him Tesla shares worth about a trillion dollars. But only if Tesla grows to be worth eight point five trillion. In February twenty twenty six, Space X merges with X A I, which already owns X. So now his rocket company also owns his social media app. Then, on June twelve, twenty twenty six, Space X starts selling shares on the stock market. It's the biggest stock market debut in history. It raises seventy five billion dollars, and by the end of the day, Space X is worth about two point one trillion. Forbes says Elon Musk is now worth about one point one trillion dollars. The first trillionaire ever.
The same year, a jury finds that two of his tweets during the Twitter deal misled investors. His lawyers say he'll appeal. And Mars? He wanted to send the first Starships there by the end of twenty twenty six. That plan has slipped. For now, Space X is aiming for the Moon first. NASA has already picked Starship to land its astronauts there.
So how does a bullied kid from Pretoria become the richest person in history? Remember the rule? He goes all in. Zip two money into X dot com. PayPal money into rockets and electric cars. forty four billion dollars into Twitter. Hundreds of millions into politics. Some of those bets go wrong. Some make history. His fans call him a genius. His critics call him reckless. But almost everyone agrees on one thing. He never bets small.
Remember that engineer on the island in two thousand eight? One more failure, and none of this would have happened.
