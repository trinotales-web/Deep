A prophecy says a wooden wall will save these men. It's going to get them killed.
It's four eighty four BC, and a huge Persian army is marching on Athens, led by King Xerxes. The Athenians ask the oracle at Delphi what to do, and the answer is strange. Only a wooden wall will not fall.
So what's the wooden wall? Most Athenians decide it means their ships. They put their families on boats and leave the city empty. But a few men stay behind, poor men and temple keepers. They take the prophecy word for word. They climb the Acropolis, the rocky hill above the city. And they block the way up with planks and old doors.
The Persians take the hill right across from them. Now, fire arrows almost never work. The flame blows out in the air, and thick walls don't catch fire. But this wall is made of dry planks and doors. So the Persians wrap their arrows in plant fiber, set them on fire, and shoot. The wooden wall burns.
The men still don't give up. They roll giant rocks down on anyone who comes near the gate.
Then a few Persians climb the steepest cliff of the hill. It's so steep, nobody thought to guard it. When the defenders see enemies on top, some jump off the walls. The rest run into the temple. Not one of them survives, and the Acropolis burns.
But remember the ships? Soon after, at Salamis, the Athenian fleet traps the Persian navy and smashes it. Xerxes watches from the shore as his ships go down.
So the oracle was right. A wooden wall did save Athens. Just not theirs.
